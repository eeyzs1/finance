Branch 06 · Risk Management

Risk Management

Risk management is not about eliminating risk — investing is, by its very nature, taking risk in exchange for return. What it really answers are three sober questions: What risk am I taking? Why am I taking it? What does it cost? This branch teaches you the common language institutions use to measure risk, and then shows you how to apply it to your own money.

After this branch, you can…

Read the VaR, expected loss, and maximum drawdown figures in institutional reports instead of being fobbed off with adjectives like “steady” and “drawdowns under control”; estimate the expected loss on a loan and work out “how high must the rate be for the loan to be worth making”; and run a stress test on your own portfolio to see in advance what extreme scenarios would do to it.

The three lessons at a glance

Lesson 1The Risk Map and VaR

Sort risks into four families — market, credit, operational, liquidity; learn VaR, the measuring language institutions share; learn to read “one-day VaR = ¥1 million at 95% confidence”, and to see what that sentence cannot tell you.

About 15 minutes · Advanced
Lesson 2Credit Risk and Expected Loss

Estimate a loan’s expected loss from “probability of default × loss given default × exposure”, then work backwards to the minimum interest rate that covers the risk.

Advanced · Lesson 1 recommended first
Lesson 3Stress Testing and Hedging

Run a stress test on a portfolio to see losses under extreme scenarios, then use hedging to transfer out the risks you don’t want to bear.

Advanced · Lesson 2 recommended first
