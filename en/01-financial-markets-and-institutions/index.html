Financial Markets & Institutions

The financial system is the infrastructure that moves funds from surplus units to deficit units: it determines which route your savings take, whose hands they pass through, whose factory or bridge they finally become — and how much “toll” each link along the way charges. This branch is the foundation of the whole course: first get a clear view of this map of funds in motion, and every branch that follows unfolds on the same map.

After this branch, you can…
  • Draw a map of funds traveling from savers to borrowers, and explain where the bank and the market each stand on it;
  • Work out a bank’s annual interest-spread income from its deposit and lending rates, and explain what direct finance saves and what it gives up;
  • Tell primary markets from secondary markets, and answer the question “when I buy stock on an exchange, does the money ever reach the company’s account?”;
  • Recognize the six types of financial intermediaries (banks, investment banks, funds, insurers, pensions, trusts & leasing), so that when a concrete financial need arises, you know whom to turn to.

Knowledge map

This branch covers six sub-areas. The first five form the backbone of its three lessons; the last (regulation and microstructure) is offered as an extension topic with credible entry points, for you to explore when you have capacity to spare.

Funds flow and financing methods
  • Who has the money (household savings) and who needs it (firms and government)
  • Indirect finance: the bank intermediates and earns the spread
  • Direct finance: securities issued on the market, funds connect directly
  • → Lesson 1: The journey of funds
Stock and bond markets
  • Stock = ownership + residual claim
  • Bond = debt claim + fixed cash flows + default risk
  • The real cost, for one company, of issuing bonds versus new shares
  • → Lesson 2: The two most basic securities
Primary and secondary markets
  • Primary market: money reaches the issuer for the first time
  • Secondary market: investors trade among themselves, gaining liquidity
  • How liquidity affects prices through the bid-ask spread
  • → Lesson 2 (core section)
Money markets and capital markets · Extension topic
  • Split by maturity: money-market instruments ≤ 1 year (interbank lending, bills, short-term bonds); capital markets > 1 year (stocks, long-term bonds)
  • Lesson 2 of this branch touches only the capital-market side; money markets wait for Branch 02, where they appear alongside central bank operations
  • Further reading: Investopedia · Money Market, Investopedia · Capital Market
Financial intermediaries
  • Commercial banks, investment banks, mutual funds/ETFs
  • Insurance companies, pension funds, trusts and leasing companies
  • Why information asymmetry makes intermediaries inevitable
  • → Lesson 3: A field guide to financial intermediaries
Financial regulation and market microstructure · Extension topic
  • Regulators (the CSRC, the central bank) set the rules and guard against systemic risk
  • Microstructure: how market makers, bid-ask spreads, and order matching determine the price you actually trade at
  • Further reading: China Securities Regulatory Commission, Wikipedia · Market Microstructure

Lessons

Lesson 1 The journey of funds: direct and indirect finance

Start from “who has money, who needs money” and see the two channels for funds: transferred through a bank (indirect finance) or connected straight through the securities market (direct finance). Comes with an interactive funds-flow animation: inject ¥100 of savings, watch it travel both paths, and see where the spread is taken out.

About 15 minutes · Includes a funds-flow simulator
Lesson 2 The two most basic securities: stock and bond markets

In the primary market money enters the company’s account; in the secondary market it merely changes hands between investors. Stock is a residual claim; a bond is a senior but fixed debt claim. A chart simulator compares the real cost, for the same company, of “issuing ¥100 million of bonds” versus “diluting by 10% with new shares.”

About 18 minutes · Includes a financing-options comparison simulator
Lesson 3 The players of the financial system: a field guide to financial intermediaries

Meet the six types of intermediaries — commercial banks, investment banks, mutual funds/ETFs, insurance companies, pension funds, trusts and leasing companies — and why information asymmetry makes them inevitable. Comes with “match the institution” self-test cards to check that you know whom to turn to.

About 15 minutes · Includes an institution-matching self-test
