Financial Markets & Institutions

Direct finance直接融资
Deficit units raise funds by selling securities (stocks and bonds) directly to surplus units in the financial markets, with no bank in between.→ Branch 01 · Lesson 1
Indirect finance间接融资
Surplus units place money with an intermediary such as a bank, which lends it on to deficit units; the intermediary itself bears the credit risk of both sides.
Primary market一级市场
The market where securities are issued and sold for the first time (e.g., IPOs and new bond issues); funds flow from investors to the issuer for the first time.
Secondary market二级市场
The market where issued securities are bought and sold among investors (stock exchanges); it does not fund the issuer directly, but it determines a security’s liquidity and pricing.
Financial intermediary金融中介
An institution that connects surplus units with deficit units: commercial banks, investment banks, mutual funds, insurance companies, pension funds, and the like.
Security证券
A transferable certificate entitling the holder to future income; the two basic kinds are stocks (ownership) and bonds (debt).→ Branch 01 · Lesson 1
Information asymmetry信息不对称
One side of a transaction knows more than the other: borrowers know best whether they will repay, while lenders cannot easily tell — the root reason financial intermediaries exist.→ Branch 01 · Lesson 1
Market-based financial system市场主导型
A financial system dominated by direct finance (the stock and bond markets), with the United States as the typical example.→ Branch 01 · Lesson 1
Bank-based financial system银行主导型
A financial system dominated by bank credit (indirect finance), with China, Germany, and Japan as typical examples.→ Branch 01 · Lesson 1
Initial public offering (IPO)首次公开募股
Initial Public Offering: a private company issues stock to public investors for the first time and lists it on an exchange.→ Branch 01 · Lesson 2
Liquidity流动性
The ability to buy or sell an asset quickly at a price close to recent transaction prices; the thinner the liquidity, the deeper the discount a forced sale must accept.→ Branch 01 · Lesson 2
Residual claim剩余索取权
Shareholders’ claim on the firm’s profits ranks after every creditor’s: revenue first pays wages, interest, and taxes, and only the remainder belongs to shareholders — uncapped at the top, possibly zero at the bottom.→ Branch 01 · Lesson 2
Coupon rate票面利率
The annual interest rate stated on a bond’s face; interest = face value × coupon rate, paid at regular intervals until maturity.→ Branch 01 · Lesson 2
Default risk违约风险
The risk that a bond issuer fails to pay interest or repay principal in full and on time — the core risk borne by bond investors.→ Branch 01 · Lesson 2
Over-the-counter (OTC) market场外市场
A market with no central trading venue, where buyers and sellers (usually dealers) negotiate prices and deal one on one, such as the interbank bond market.→ Branch 01 · Lesson 2
Bid-ask spread买卖价差
The gap, at a given moment, between the highest price buyers will pay (the bid) and the lowest price sellers will accept (the ask) — a direct gauge of liquidity.→ Branch 01 · Lesson 2
Screening甄别
Investigating and sorting loan or insurance applicants before funds change hands, separating good borrowers from bad and countering ex-ante adverse selection.→ Branch 01 · Lesson 3
Monitoring监督
After funds reach the borrower, the ongoing work of tracking operations and the use of funds, collecting payments, and restructuring — countering ex-post moral hazard.→ Branch 01 · Lesson 3
Risk pooling风险汇聚
Bringing the separate uncertain risks of many individuals together and handling them in bulk, so the aggregate outcome becomes more predictable and the cost per unit of risk falls.→ Branch 01 · Lesson 3
Maturity transformation期限转换
Banks convert savers’ short-term deposits, withdrawable at any time, into long-term loans for firms; the reward is the maturity spread, the cost is exposure to bank runs.→ Branch 01 · Lesson 3
Underwriting承销
Investment banks place newly issued securities on a firm-commitment or best-efforts basis, bearing the selling risk and charging an underwriting fee; pricing and selling IPOs is the core of the work.→ Branch 01 · Lesson 3
ETF
An exchange-traded fund tracks an index and trades on the exchange like a stock, with low fees and high transparency.→ Branch 01 · Lesson 3

Money & Banking

Money supply aggregates (M0/M1/M2)货币供应量（M0/M1/M2）
M0 = currency in circulation; M1 = M0 + demand deposits (money spendable at any time); M2 = M1 + time deposits and other quasi-money (potential purchasing power). The central bank watches M2 to gauge total purchasing power in the economy.
Required reserve ratio存款准备金率
Funds banks are legally required to hold as a percentage of deposits and not lend out. The lower the ratio, the more banks can lend and the larger the money multiplier.→ Branch 02 · Lesson 2
Money multiplier货币乘数
The multiple by which one unit of monetary base is ultimately amplified through repeated rounds of deposits and loans in the banking system; the theoretical ceiling is roughly 1/reserve ratio.
Monetary policy货币政策
The policy by which the central bank uses tools such as interest rates, the reserve ratio, and open market operations to regulate money and credit, and thereby prices and employment.
Policy rate政策利率
The key rate a central bank sets directly or steers (e.g., the LPR/MLF rates in China, the federal funds rate in the United States); the anchor of the economy’s whole rate system.
Money货币
Anything widely accepted in payment for goods and services or in the repayment of debts.→ Branch 02 · Lesson 1
Medium of exchange交易媒介
An intermediate object both buyers and sellers accept, so that exchange no longer requires barter.→ Branch 02 · Lesson 1
Unit of account价值尺度
A common yardstick that expresses the value of all goods in one unit.→ Branch 02 · Lesson 1
Store of value价值储藏
The ability to carry today’s purchasing power into the future.→ Branch 02 · Lesson 1
Double coincidence of wants需求双重巧合
The precondition of a barter deal: each side happens to want exactly what the other has.→ Branch 02 · Lesson 1
Derivative deposit派生存款
A deposit formed when a bank’s lending flows back into the banking system, in contrast to the original deposit first placed.→ Branch 02 · Lesson 2
Monetary base基础货币
Currency issued by the central bank plus the reserves banks hold at the central bank — the original ammunition of money creation.→ Branch 02 · Lesson 2
Central bank中央银行
The institution in charge of issuing a country’s currency, safeguarding financial stability, and providing clearing and liquidity support to the banking system.→ Branch 02 · Lesson 3
Lender of last resort最后贷款人
The central bank’s role of supplying emergency liquidity when banks are strapped and no one else will lend.→ Branch 02 · Lesson 3
Open market operations公开市场操作
Operations in which the central bank buys and sells government bonds, central bank bills, and the like in the open market to manage liquidity.→ Branch 02 · Lesson 3

Corporate Finance

Time value of money货币时间价值
A dollar today is worth more than a dollar tomorrow, because it can be invested immediately to earn a return. The foundation of all valuation.
Discount rate折现率
The rate used to convert future cash flows into today’s value; it includes the opportunity cost of funds and compensation for risk.
Net present value (NPV)净现值（NPV）
The sum of future cash flows discounted at the discount rate, minus the initial investment. NPV>0 means value is created and the project is worth taking.→ Branch 03 · Lesson 1
Capital structure资本结构
The mix of debt and equity in a firm’s funding. Leverage magnifies gains as well as losses.
Weighted average cost of capital (WACC)加权平均资本成本（WACC）
The cost of financing weighted by the market-value shares of debt and equity; commonly used as the discount rate for the firm’s cash flows.
Price-to-earnings ratio (P/E)市盈率（P/E）
Share price ÷ earnings per share — a rough gauge of “how many years to earn your money back at current earnings,” and the most widely used relative valuation multiple.
Future value终值
The value of today’s money at a future date when carried forward at the interest rate; written FV.→ Branch 03 · Lesson 1
Present value现值
What future money is worth after being discounted to today; written PV.→ Branch 03 · Lesson 1
Internal rate of return (IRR)内含报酬率
The discount rate at which NPV is exactly zero; the project’s intrinsic break-even rate of return.→ Branch 03 · Lesson 1
Tax shield税盾
The tax saving created because interest is deductible before taxable income is computed.→ Branch 03 · Lesson 2
Financial leverage财务杠杆
The mechanism by which fixed-cost debt magnifies shareholders’ returns and risks.→ Branch 03 · Lesson 2
Financial distress costs财务困境成本
The costs that surface as default risk rises — legal fees, fire sales of assets, and lost customers.→ Branch 03 · Lesson 2
Valuation估值
Converting a company’s future rewards and risks into a price today.→ Branch 03 · Lesson 3
Price-to-book ratio (P/B)市净率
Share price divided by book value per share, measuring what you pay for every ¥1 of book net assets.→ Branch 03 · Lesson 3
EV/EBITDA
Enterprise value divided by earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization — a valuation yardstick that compares companies more fairly.→ Branch 03 · Lesson 3
Discounted cash flow (DCF)现金流折现
Discounting future free cash flows period by period and summing them to get the company’s value today.→ Branch 03 · Lesson 3
Gordon growth model戈登增长模型
The infinite-horizon discounting formula for steadily growing dividends, V=D1/(r−g).→ Branch 03 · Lesson 3

Investments

Rate of return收益率
(Ending value − beginning value + interim income) ÷ beginning value. In investments, “return” almost always means a ratio, not an absolute amount.
Volatility (standard deviation)波动率（标准差）
How far returns deviate from their average — the most common measure of risk: the greater the volatility, the more uncertain the outcome.
Diversification分散投资
Allocating funds across many assets with low correlations, reducing portfolio volatility without sacrificing expected return.
Correlation coefficient相关系数
The degree to which two assets’ returns move together, ranging from −1 to +1. The closer to −1, the better the two offset each other’s risk in combination.
Beta (β)贝塔系数（β）
The sensitivity of a stock’s return to the market portfolio’s return. β=1.2 means that when the market rises 1%, the stock rises 1.2% on average.
Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM)资本资产定价模型（CAPM）
Expected return = risk-free rate + β × market risk premium: only undiversifiable systematic risk deserves compensation.→ Branch 04 · Lesson 3
Efficient frontier有效前沿
The curve traced out by the portfolios with the highest expected return at each level of risk; rational investors choose only points on this line.
Expected return期望收益
The average return obtained by weighting each possible outcome by its probability of occurring.→ Branch 04 · Lesson 1
Geometric mean几何平均
The true average annual return, computed by compounding the returns and then taking the corresponding root.→ Branch 04 · Lesson 1
Idiosyncratic risk特质风险
Risk unique to a single asset that diversification can eliminate; the counterpart of systematic risk.→ Branch 04 · Lesson 2
Systematic risk系统性风险
Risk shared by the market as a whole that diversification cannot remove; the market pays compensation only for it.→ Branch 04 · Lesson 3
Security Market Line (SML)证券市场线
The pricing line with β on the horizontal axis and expected return on the vertical: each β corresponds to the expected return it “should” offer.→ Branch 04 · Lesson 3
Market portfolio市场组合
The portfolio holding every risky asset at market-value weights; the sum of all investors’ holdings, which CAPM places on the efficient frontier.→ Branch 04 · Lesson 3

Derivatives & Financial Engineering

Forward contract远期合约
A customized contract in which two parties agree to deliver an asset on a future date at a price set today; both sides must perform at maturity.
Futures contract期货
An exchange-traded, standardized forward contract settled daily; its main uses are hedging and speculation.
Option期权
A “right, not an obligation” bought by paying a premium: a call allows buying at the strike price, a put allows selling.
Strike price行权价
The buy or sell price fixed in the option contract — the dividing line that determines whether the option has value at expiry.
Premium权利金
The price paid to buy an option = intrinsic value + time value; it is the option buyer’s maximum loss and the seller’s maximum gain.
Call option / put option看涨期权 / 看跌期权
A call option: the right to buy the underlying at the strike price (a bet on a rise); a put option: the right to sell the underlying at the strike price (a bet on a fall, or insurance for a holding).
Intrinsic value and time value内在价值与时间价值
Intrinsic value = what exercising immediately would bring (it exists only in the money); time value = the part of the premium above intrinsic value, which comes from the chance of price moves before expiry and decays with time.
Break-even point盈亏平衡点
The underlying price at which an option strategy neither gains nor loses: long call = strike + premium; long put = strike − premium.
Marking to market每日盯市
The mechanism by which futures gains and losses are settled daily at the closing price and transferred the same day; together with margin, it keeps losses from piling up into default.
Margin保证金
A small amount of collateral a trader posts to guarantee performance; if losses push it below the maintenance line, the trader must top it up or be force-liquidated.
Binomial option pricing model二叉树模型
A pricing method that assumes the underlying can only move up or down over the next period and works back to the option’s fair value with risk-neutral probabilities; extended over many steps it approaches the continuous model.
Risk-neutral probability风险中性概率
The hypothetical probability that makes the underlying’s expected return equal to the risk-free rate, p=(1+r−d)/(u−d); used only for pricing, it is not the real probability of a rise or fall.
Black-Scholes model布莱克-斯科尔斯模型
The classic continuous-time option pricing formula; its idea is to replicate the option’s payoff dynamically with the underlying asset and risk-free bonds.
Contango / backwardation贴水与升水
A futures price below spot is called backwardation, and above spot contango; in forward exchange rates, the higher-interest foreign currency trades at a forward discount, as interest rate parity dictates.
Stock index futures discount股指期货贴水
The state in which stock index futures trade below the underlying spot index, often reflecting dividends and hedging demand; above spot is a premium.→ Branch 05 · Branch Overview
Payoff diagram盈亏图
A line chart with the underlying’s price at expiry on the horizontal axis and the position’s profit or loss on the vertical — the common language of the options world.→ Branch 05 · Branch Overview
Swap互换
A contract in which two parties agree to exchange cash flows in the future (e.g., a fixed-for-floating interest rate swap), used by banks to manage and transfer interest rate risk.
Hedging套期保值
Using derivatives to take a position opposite to an existing exposure, locking in prices and removing the effect of fluctuations.
Derivative衍生品
A contract whose value depends on (derives from) the price of an underlying asset — e.g., forwards, futures, options, and swaps.→ Branch 05 · Lesson 1
Volatility smile波动率微笑
The U-shaped curve traced by the implied volatilities backed out of options sharing one expiry but different strikes, higher at the deep in-the-money and out-of-the-money ends.→ Branch 05 · Lesson 3
Implied volatility隐含波动率
The underlying’s volatility backed out of the option’s market price — the market’s own pricing of volatility.→ Branch 05 · Lesson 3

Risk Management

Value at Risk (VaR)在险价值（VaR）
The maximum likely loss over a given horizon at a given confidence level. E.g., “95% 1-day VaR = ¥1 million”: there is a 5% chance of losing more than ¥1 million in a single day.
Expected loss (EL)预期损失（EL）
The core formula of credit risk: EL = PD × LGD × EAD (probability of default × loss given default × exposure at default).
Probability of default (PD)违约概率（PD）
The likelihood that a borrower defaults within a given period; the core quantity credit ratings aim to measure.
Stress testing压力测试
A tool that poses extreme but plausible scenarios (rates +200bp, home prices down 30%) and tests whether a portfolio or institution can survive them.
Market risk市场风险
The risk of asset values rising and falling with price movements.→ Branch 06 · Lesson 1
Credit risk信用风险
The risk that a counterparty fails to perform at maturity and does not pay back.→ Branch 06 · Lesson 1
Operational risk操作风险
The risk of loss from people, processes, or systems spinning out of control.→ Branch 06 · Lesson 1
Liquidity risk流动性风险
The risk of wanting to sell but finding no buyer, so the sale goes through only at a steep discount.→ Branch 06 · Lesson 1
Loss given default (LGD)违约损失率
The share of the exposure ultimately lost after a default; the more ample the collateral, the lower the ratio.→ Branch 06 · Lesson 2
Exposure at default (EAD)违约风险敞口
The amount still outstanding and at risk when default occurs.→ Branch 06 · Lesson 2
Credit rating信用评级
A rating agency’s grade of how likely a borrower is to default, running from AAA down to D.→ Branch 06 · Lesson 2
Drawdown回撤
The largest fall in a portfolio’s value from a peak — a measure of the most painful stretch of loss.→ Branch 06 · Lesson 3
Scenario analysis情景分析
Selecting or constructing specific scenarios, assuming prices asset by asset, and computing the portfolio’s profit and loss.→ Branch 06 · Lesson 3
Capital buffer资本缓冲
A loss-absorbing layer of highly liquid assets such as cash, held to absorb shocks and avoid forced selling.→ Branch 06 · Lesson 3

International Finance

Exchange rate汇率
The price of one currency in terms of another; e.g., USD/CNY=7.2 means 1 US dollar exchanges for ¥7.2.
Purchasing power parity (PPP)购买力平价
The idea that the same basket of goods should cost the same in every country; the center of gravity for long-run exchange rates (the Big Mac Index is its popular version).
Interest rate parity利率平价
A higher-interest currency should trade at a forward discount and a lower-interest currency at a forward premium; otherwise a risk-free arbitrage exists.
Balance of payments (BOP)国际收支（BOP）
The record of all transactions between a country’s residents and the rest of the world: current account + capital and financial account + reserve assets — it must balance as a whole.
Direct quotation直接标价法
Quoting the exchange rate as the amount of home currency per unit of foreign currency (the foreign unit is fixed at 1); China uses this convention.→ Branch 07 · Lesson 1
Appreciation升值
A currency exchanges for more of another currency — it has become more valuable.→ Branch 07 · Lesson 1
Depreciation贬值
A currency exchanges for less of another currency — it has become less valuable.→ Branch 07 · Lesson 1
Nominal exchange rate名义汇率
The exchange rate as quoted by banks, unadjusted for the two countries’ prices.→ Branch 07 · Lesson 1
Real exchange rate实际汇率
The nominal rate adjusted for the price levels of the two countries, reflecting the true comparison of purchasing power.→ Branch 07 · Lesson 1
Law of one price一价定律
When trade is free and transport costs and barriers are ignored, the same good should have the same price in every country once converted into a common currency.→ Branch 07 · Lesson 2
Big Mac Index巨无霸指数
A popular indicator compiled by The Economist that uses the ratio of Big Mac prices across countries to roughly estimate the PPP exchange rate.→ Branch 07 · Lesson 2
Forward rate远期汇率
The exchange rate agreed today for delivering foreign exchange on a specified future date.→ Branch 07 · Lesson 2
Current account经常账户
The account recording flows of real resources: goods, services, primary income, and current transfers.→ Branch 07 · Lesson 3
Capital and financial account资本与金融账户
The account recording cross-border purchases and sales of assets: direct investment, portfolio investment, and other investment.→ Branch 07 · Lesson 3
Reserve assets储备资产
Changes in the official reserve assets — foreign exchange, gold, and the like — held by the central bank.→ Branch 07 · Lesson 3
Foreign direct investment (FDI)直接投资
Investment that acquires lasting control of an enterprise in another country, by building plants or acquiring businesses; recorded in the capital and financial account.→ Branch 07 · Lesson 3

Behavioral Finance

Loss aversion损失厌恶
The pain of a loss is roughly 2–2.5 times the pleasure of an equal gain (the core finding of prospect theory).
Prospect theory前景理论
Kahneman and Tversky’s model of decision making: people evaluate risk by gains and losses relative to a reference point (not by final wealth) and overweight small-probability extremes.
Anchoring锚定效应
The first number seen drags later judgments toward it, even when the number carries no information.
Herding羊群效应
Copying other people’s actions instead of judging independently — the collective psychology behind bubbles and stampedes.
Disposition effect处置效应
Investors’ tendency to sell winning positions in a hurry while clinging to losing positions — a direct product of loss aversion.
Certainty effect确定性效应
People systematically overvalue outcomes that are 100% certain, giving up uncertain options with higher expected value just to be sure.→ Branch 08 · Lesson 1
Reference point参照点
The benchmark against which gains and losses are measured: usually expectations, the status quo, or the purchase price — not the level of final wealth.→ Branch 08 · Lesson 1
Availability heuristic可得性启发
The mental shortcut of judging probability by how easily examples come to mind.→ Branch 08 · Lesson 2
Overconfidence过度自信
The tendency to overestimate the accuracy of one’s judgments and the quality of one’s information.→ Branch 08 · Lesson 2
Confirmation bias确认偏差
The tendency to look for and remember only the evidence that supports what one already believes.→ Branch 08 · Lesson 2
Mental accounting心理账户
The tendency to label money in one’s head by its source and treat it differently according to the label.→ Branch 08 · Lesson 2
Bubble泡沫
A market state in which prices run far beyond what fundamentals support and depend on a constant stream of new buyers.→ Branch 08 · Lesson 3
Momentum动量效应
The phenomenon that winners over the past 3–12 months keep outperforming losers over the short run.→ Branch 08 · Lesson 3
Mean reversion均值回归
The tendency of extreme performance to fall back toward the average over the long run.→ Branch 08 · Lesson 3
Investment policy statement (IPS)投资政策声明
A written document, drawn up in advance, of investment goals, rules, and discipline.→ Branch 08 · Lesson 3

Insurance & Actuarial Science

Law of large numbers大数法则
The more independent, identically distributed risk events are pooled, the more stable the average loss becomes, converging on the expected value — the mathematical foundation that makes insurance feasible.
Pure premium纯保费
The premium that just covers expected claims = sum insured × probability of loss; the actual premium adds loadings for expenses and profit.
Adverse selection逆向选择
Under information asymmetry, high-risk customers are the most willing to insure, so the risk pool steadily worsens and premiums spiral upward.
Moral hazard道德风险
Behavior changes once insured (people take less care), pushing the actual probability of loss above the pricing assumption.
Actuarial science精算
The discipline that prices risk and insurance with mathematics and statistics.→ Branch 09 · Lesson 1
Monte Carlo simulation蒙特卡洛模拟
A simulation method that approaches an answer through the statistics of a large number of random trials.→ Branch 09 · Lesson 1
Reinsurance再保险
An arrangement by which an insurer transfers risk it has already underwritten to other insurers.→ Branch 09 · Lesson 1
Loading附加保费
The portion of the premium that covers operating costs, profit, and risk reserves, commonly charged as a set percentage of the pure premium.→ Branch 09 · Lesson 2
Risk classification风险分类
The practice of grouping policyholders by their probability of loss and pricing each group separately.→ Branch 09 · Lesson 2
Deductible免赔额
The amount of a loss that the insured bears personally and the insurer does not pay.→ Branch 09 · Lesson 2
Coinsurance共付比例
The insured’s share of a loss above the deductible, with the two sides splitting the cost by a set ratio.→ Branch 09 · Lesson 2
Death spiral死亡螺旋
A self-reinforcing cycle in which the risk pool keeps deteriorating and premiums keep rising until the market falls apart.→ Branch 09 · Lesson 3
Underwriting核保
The insurer’s process of reviewing risk information such as health and occupation before deciding whether to insure the applicant and at what rate.→ Branch 09 · Lesson 3

Personal Finance

Compound interest复利
Interest earning interest: FV = PV×(1+r)^n. Time is the only lever of compounding.
Inflation通货膨胀
A sustained rise in the price level and a sustained fall in the purchasing power of money; the true benchmark for personal finance is “nominal return − inflation.”
Asset allocation资产配置
How funds are distributed across major asset classes such as stocks, bonds, and cash; research shows it explains most of the differences in portfolios’ long-run returns.
Emergency fund应急基金
A highly liquid reserve covering 3–6 months of expenses — the first line of defense, in place before any investing.
Dollar-cost averaging定投
Buying a fixed amount of an asset at regular intervals, spreading purchase prices over time to overcome the difficulty of timing and emotional interference.
Simple interest单利
Interest calculated only on the original principal, never folded back into principal to earn more interest.→ Branch 10 · Lesson 1
Rule of 7272 法则
A mental-math tool: divide 72 by the annual rate of return in percent to estimate how many years it takes for principal to double.→ Branch 10 · Lesson 1
Real return真实收益率
The growth in purchasing power after removing inflation, approximately the nominal return minus the inflation rate.→ Branch 10 · Lesson 1
Risk tolerance风险承受能力
How much volatility and loss one can withstand, financially and psychologically.→ Branch 10 · Lesson 2
Target-date fund目标日期基金
A fund named for its target retirement year that automatically lowers its stock allocation as the date approaches.→ Branch 10 · Lesson 2
Rebalancing再平衡
Periodically buying and selling assets to pull a portfolio that has drifted back to its target weights.→ Branch 10 · Lesson 2
Amortization (equal installments)等额本息
A repayment method with a fixed monthly installment: interest takes a larger share of the payment early on, principal a larger share later.→ Branch 10 · Lesson 3
Debt avalanche method雪崩法
Paying off the highest-interest-rate debt first — the payoff order that minimizes total interest.→ Branch 10 · Lesson 3
Debt snowball method雪球法
Paying off the smallest-balance debt first — the payoff order that delivers the quickest sense of progress.→ Branch 10 · Lesson 3

FinTech

Blockchain区块链
A distributed ledger technology chained together by cryptographic hashes, maintained jointly by many parties, and extremely hard to tamper with.
Central bank digital currency (CBDC)央行数字货币
Legal tender in digital form issued directly by the central bank (e.g., the digital yuan, e-CNY); fundamentally different in nature from crypto assets.
Robo-advisor智能投顾
A digital advisory service that handles risk profiling, asset allocation, and rebalancing by algorithm, at a far lower cost than human advisers.
Payments and clearing支付清算
The reconciliation and settlement system that carries transaction funds from payer to payee (the “four-party model”: merchant, acquirer, card network, issuer).
Clearing清算
After a transaction occurs, the process in which the parties check messages and net out who owes whom how much — before any funds actually move.→ Branch 11 · Lesson 1
Dual offline payment双离线支付
A payment that can be completed even when neither payer nor payee has network access, with the accounts synced once back online.→ Branch 11 · Lesson 1
Acquirer收单机构
The institution that provides merchants with payment-acceptance services and forwards transactions to the clearing network.→ Branch 11 · Lesson 1
Card network卡组织
The network connecting issuers and acquirers and setting the routing and clearing rules — e.g., UnionPay, NetsUnion, and the international card networks.→ Branch 11 · Lesson 1
Settlement结算
The step in which funds actually move according to the clearing result — the money truly changes hands.→ Branch 11 · Lesson 1
Hash哈希
A function that turns any data into a fixed-length fingerprint; change one character and the output changes completely.→ Branch 11 · Lesson 2
Consensus mechanism共识机制
The rules by which a distributed network reaches agreement on whose recorded entry counts as valid.→ Branch 11 · Lesson 2
Proof of work工作量证明
A consensus mechanism in which computing power competes for the right to record entries; it consumes a great deal of electricity.→ Branch 11 · Lesson 2
Stablecoin稳定币
A crypto asset pegged to fiat currency or other assets, aiming to hold its price steady.→ Branch 11 · Lesson 2
Credit scoring信贷评分
A system that uses models to score how likely a borrower is to default.→ Branch 11 · Lesson 3
Alternative data替代数据
New data sources beyond traditional bank statements and collateral, such as consumption and behavioral records.→ Branch 11 · Lesson 3
Algorithmic bias算法偏见
The phenomenon of a model carrying forward — even amplifying — the historical discrimination embedded in its training data.→ Branch 11 · Lesson 3
