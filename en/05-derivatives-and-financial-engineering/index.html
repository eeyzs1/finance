Derivatives & Financial Engineering

Derivatives are “contracts about the future”: they produce nothing themselves, and one party’s gain is necessarily the other party’s loss (zero-sum). Nor does their value come from within — it is entirely “derived” from the underlying asset they are tied to: oil, soybeans, stocks, indexes, interest rates — almost anything can serve as the underlying.

After this branch, you can…
  • decode what financial news is really saying when it mentions “stock index futures discount”;
  • understand why options have a price at all, and what makes them more or less expensive;
  • use the payoff diagram to draw any simple option strategy (protective puts, covered calls, …).

Knowledge map

The backbone of this branch is two lessons on the derivative “building blocks” (futures, options) plus one lesson on the intuition of pricing; swaps, structured products, and quant practice are extension directions the course does not develop, but we leave you signposts.

Forwards & Futures
  • Forwards vs. futures: customization vs. standardization
  • Marking to market and the margin system
  • Hedging: locking in tomorrow’s price today
Options
  • Calls/puts × buyers/sellers: four basic positions
  • Payoff diagrams at expiration and break-even points
  • Intrinsic value and time value
Swaps (extension topic)
  • Contracts that agree to exchange cash flows in the future
  • Interest rate swaps and currency swaps are the most common
  • Further reading: Investopedia: Swap
Pricing Theory
  • The one-step binomial tree and risk-neutral probability
  • The idea behind the Black-Scholes (BS) model
  • Volatility: the heartbeat of option prices
Structured Products (extension topic)
  • Wealth-management products assembled from bonds + options
  • What “principal protection + a shot at high returns” really costs
  • Best revisited after Lessons 2 and 3
Quant & Financial Engineering Practice (extension topic)
  • Pricing, hedging, and market-making with math and code
  • Volatility trading and derivatives risk management
  • A starter map of related career directions

Lessons

Lesson 1 Futures and Hedging

How does an airline use futures to “settle today” the price of jet fuel it will need three months from now? Forwards versus futures, marking to market, margin, and why speculators are indispensable.

Includes a hedging simulator · 4-question quiz
Lesson 2 · Signature lesson Option Payoff Structures

A right, not an obligation: payoff diagrams at expiration for the four positions formed by calls/puts × buyers/sellers, break-even points, intrinsic and time value, plus the protective put — buying “insurance” for your stock.

Includes an option payoff diagram generator · 4-question quiz
Lesson 3 The Intuition Behind Option Pricing

Why do bigger swings and more time make an option more expensive? A one-step binomial tree works a complete numerical example step by step, then we meet the “replication” idea behind the Black-Scholes model.

Includes a binomial tree calculator · 4-question quiz

Where this sits in your learning path

You have just come from Behavioral Finance — you already know how systematically people go astray when money is on the line. Derivatives happen to be the arena with the hardest rules and the harshest leverage, holding behavioral biases under a magnifying glass. The next stop, Risk Management, flips this branch’s tools around: the goal is not to make money, but to survive.
