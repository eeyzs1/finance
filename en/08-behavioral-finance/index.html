Behavioral Finance

Traditional finance assumes a “rational man”: someone who coolly computes expected values and always maximizes utility. Behavioral finance studies real people — people who act on impulse, feel regret, and follow the crowd. The key finding of this branch is that deviations from rationality are not random noise but systematic, directionally predictable biases. Two Nobel Prizes in Economic Sciences — in 2002 (Kahneman) and 2017 (Thaler) — have gone to this field.

After this branch, you can…

① Spot the three biases most likely to trip up your money decisions (loss aversion, overconfidence, herding); ② explain the psychological roots of “selling winners too soon and clinging to losers too long”; ③ build a bias-proof set of investing rules that turns decisions from “gut feeling in the moment” into “discipline written down in advance”.

Branch knowledge map

Prospect theory and loss aversion
  • Gains and losses are judged relative to a “reference point,” not final wealth
  • Losses hurt roughly 2.25 times as much as equal-sized gains feel good
  • The S-shaped value function and probability weighting
  • → Lesson 1
Heuristics and cognitive biases
  • Anchoring: judgments tethered to the first number
  • Availability: the easier something is to recall, the more common it seems
  • Representativeness: mistaking “resembles” for “is”
  • → Lesson 2
Overconfidence and trading behavior
  • Overconfidence: overrating your judgment, underrating luck
  • The illusion of control and hindsight bias
  • Why trading harder earns you less
  • → Lesson 2
Herding and bubbles
  • Conformity: if everyone else is buying, I buy too
  • Information cascades and self-reinforcing bubbles
  • Historical cases, from tulips to the Internet
  • → Lesson 3
Market anomalies and limits to arbitrage
  • Momentum and reversal: prices are not fully random
  • Calendar effects and the size effect
  • Why even “smart money” can’t stamp out anomalies
  • → Lesson 3
Neurofinance and experimental economics
  • Testing finance theories in laboratory experiments
  • Watching the brain decide with neuroimaging
  • Further reading: Neuroeconomics (Wikipedia)

Lessons in this branch

How this branch connects

The previous branch, 03 Corporate Finance, taught you how to value projects and companies — and the prettier the formula, the more you should watch the person applying it; behavioral finance studies exactly that “person using the formula.” When you finish this branch, continue to 05 Derivatives & Financial Engineering: however refined the pricing and hedging models, they are still executed by fallible human beings, and understanding bias will help you use those powerful tools more safely.
